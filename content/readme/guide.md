### What the library measures

Those patterns are not a house style; they are what this corpus does. The median prompt here is {{MEDIAN_PROMPT_CHARS}} characters — the shortest {{SHORTEST_PROMPT_CHARS}}, the longest {{LONGEST_PROMPT_CHARS}}, {{TOTAL_PROMPT_CHARS}} characters of prompt text in all. That median is the most useful number on this page: a prompt that reproduces for this model is closer to a short brief than to a sentence, and most people arrive expecting the opposite.

The long ones are not longer sentences. The longest quarter of the library — {{LONGEST_QUARTILE_COUNT}} prompts, from {{LONGEST_QUARTILE_MIN_CHARS}} characters up — runs to a median of {{LONGEST_QUARTILE_LINE_MEDIAN}} lines each, against {{SHORTEST_QUARTILE_LINE_MEDIAN}} in the shortest quarter, and {{MULTILINE_COUNT}} of the {{ROW_COUNT}} are broken across lines at all. They get long by adding parts rather than clauses: roughly one line per decision.

What that length is spent on is measurable, and it is not adjectives. {{FORMAT_COUNT}} of the {{ROW_COUNT}} name an explicit aspect ratio or page size. {{QUOTED_TEXT_COUNT}} of the {{PROSE_COUNT}} written as prose quote at least one literal string for the model to render. {{CAMERA_COUNT}} name a lens, an aperture, a camera angle or a depth-of-field instruction, and they cluster where a result has to be repeatable rather than merely good — heaviest in the {{CAMERA_TOP_CATEGORY_LABEL}} file, at {{CAMERA_TOP_CATEGORY_COUNT}} of {{CAMERA_TOP_CATEGORY_TOTAL}}. {{EXCLUSION_COUNT}} end on an explicit exclusion clause. And colour is named in words rather than values: {{HEX_COLOUR_ROWS}} of the {{ROW_COUNT}} give a hex code.
